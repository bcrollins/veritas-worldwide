# Israel Dossier Chapter Draft - Source-Boundary Edition

Last source check: 2026-04-22

Working title: Public Records, Reported Harm, and Legal Posture

## Editorial Boundary

This draft is built from the populated Israel dossier workbooks. It is not a final legal finding, not a casualty adjudication, and not a substitute for source review. Each paragraph preserves source-row IDs so an editor can trace numbers, incident language, and legal posture back to the workbook file before publication.

The chapter can be published only if the source boundary stays visible. Public records can establish appropriations, dates, institutional actions, reported figures, source classes, and procedural posture. They cannot, by themselves, prove every delivery chain, every incident sequence, individual criminal liability, or final judicial outcome.

## 1. The Financial Floor

Source rows: SRC-P-001, AID-P-001, AID-P-002, AID-P-003, AID-P-004

Paragraph source IDs: SRC-P-001, AID-P-001, AID-P-002

The financial record begins with a public-record floor, not with an accusation. The Congressional Research Service identifies Israel as the largest cumulative recipient of U.S. foreign assistance since World War II and estimates inflation-adjusted U.S. aid obligations at $298 billion from 1946 through 2024. CRS also reports current-dollar assistance and missile-defense totals in separate accounting frames. Those frames matter. Inflation-adjusted cumulative obligations, current-dollar totals, missile-defense lines, and annual appropriations are related records, but they are not interchangeable figures.

Paragraph source IDs: AID-P-001, AID-P-002

The safest opening claim is therefore narrow: the public record establishes the scale and continuity of U.S. support. It can say that CRS provides a cumulative obligations floor and that the aid ledger carries that figure into a source-reviewed workbook. It should not imply that the $298 billion figure is a delivery log, a weapon-transfer record, or a causation record for any particular incident. The money record is the base layer. It establishes the institutional floor on which later questions can be asked.

Paragraph source IDs: AID-P-003, AID-P-004

The recent appropriations record should remain distinct from the long-run cumulative figure. The 2016 memorandum of understanding supplies a $3.8 billion annual military-aid baseline for FY2019 through FY2028. H.R.815 added a $26.4 billion Israel security supplemental in April 2024, including security, procurement, and missile-defense categories. Those records establish congressional authorization and scale. They do not, by themselves, establish delivery timing, end use, or direct causation in a named incident.

Paragraph source IDs: SRC-P-001, AID-P-004

This distinction is the chapter's first guardrail. A reader should be able to see why the financial record belongs in the dossier while also seeing what the financial record cannot prove. If a later paragraph discusses humanitarian harm or a specific strike, the chapter must not silently move from "funding record" to "incident causation." That bridge requires delivery records, end-use records, weapon-transfer records, or official findings that tie a particular appropriation to a particular event.

Unsafe wording to avoid: Do not write that a specific death, convoy strike, shelter strike, or reconstruction was paid for by a specific appropriation unless a delivery record, weapon-transfer record, and end-use record establish that chain.

Section QA checklist:

- Keep CRS RL33222 and H.R.815 as separate source rows.
- Keep constant-dollar, current-dollar, annual baseline, and supplemental figures in separate sentences unless the source itself combines them.
- Do not turn an appropriation into proof of delivery or incident causation.

## 2. Reported Humanitarian Harm

Source rows: HUM-P-001, HUM-P-002, HUM-P-003, HUM-P-004, HUM-P-005, HUM-P-006

Paragraph source IDs: HUM-P-001, HUM-P-002, HUM-P-003

The humanitarian record is substantial, but the source classes are not identical. OCHA reported 72,289 Palestinian fatalities in Gaza as of 1 April 2026, attributed to MoH Gaza. UNICEF reported 21,289 children killed and 44,500 children injured through 3 February 2026. Those are reported figures with named attribution chains and date ranges. They should be written as reported figures, not as free-floating totals detached from the reporting body.

Paragraph source IDs: HUM-P-001

The OCHA row is an administrative reporting chain. It can support the sentence that OCHA reported a fatality figure as of a stated date and attributed the figure to MoH Gaza. It does not prove that every underlying death record has been independently verified by OCHA. If the chapter quotes the number, the source, date, and attribution chain should remain in the same paragraph or footnote.

Paragraph source IDs: HUM-P-002, HUM-P-003

The UNICEF rows add a narrower child-harm layer. They can support the sentence that UNICEF reported 21,289 children killed and 44,500 children injured through 3 February 2026. Those figures should not be separated from the UNICEF update date or converted into a final court finding. If the prose compares those figures to another agency or monitor, the comparison must name the different reporting bodies and dates.

Paragraph source IDs: HUM-P-004, HUM-P-005

Survey estimates and verified samples need their own labels. A Lancet Global Health survey estimated 75,200 violent deaths through 5 January 2025; that figure should be described as a survey estimate, not a live administrative count. OHCHR reported that women and children made up 70 percent of verified fatalities in homes and residential buildings in its sample; that sample boundary has to remain visible. The denominator and sample frame are part of the claim.

Paragraph source IDs: HUM-P-001, HUM-P-004, HUM-P-005

The publication-ready paragraph can therefore say that multiple records point to large-scale reported harm, but it must keep each record's method visible. OCHA reports an attributed administrative figure. UNICEF reports child fatality and injury figures through a dated update. The Lancet row is a survey estimate. The OHCHR row is a verified sample. The chapter should not flatten those categories into one number because doing so would make the record appear more certain than the sources allow.

Unsafe wording to avoid: Do not strip attribution from reported figures, and do not present a survey estimate, a monitor tracker, and an administrative report as one undifferentiated count.

Section QA checklist:

- Keep OCHA/MoH attribution attached to OCHA reported figures.
- Keep UNICEF child fatality and injury figures paired with the 3 February 2026 date range.
- Label the Lancet row as a survey estimate.
- Label the OHCHR row as a verified sample and preserve the sample boundary.

## 3. Incident Evidence

Source rows: INC-P-001, INC-P-002, INC-P-003, INC-P-004, INC-P-005

Paragraph source IDs: INC-P-001, INC-P-003

The incident file can support narrative detail if the evidence type stays attached to each sentence. Forensic Architecture reconstructed the killing of Hind Rajab and reported evidence consistent with fire at close range. That supports forensic-reconstruction language. It does not, without a competent legal finding, establish individual criminal liability. The OHCHR residential-building sample can support a sentence about a verified sample of fatalities in homes and residential buildings, but the sample frame must remain part of the sentence.

Paragraph source IDs: INC-P-002

World Central Kitchen said seven aid workers were killed after its convoy was struck in Gaza. That row can be used to explain why aid-worker protection appears in the dossier, and it can support the casualty figure as reported by WCK. It should not be rewritten as a final legal finding on motive or individual culpability unless a separate adjudicative source is added.

Paragraph source IDs: INC-P-004

OCHA reported recovery of bodies from PRCS, Palestinian Civil Defense, and UN personnel in Rafah after the March 2025 paramedic convoy killings. This row can support careful narrative language about the recovery record and the institutions named in the report. It should not collapse the sequence, disputed elements, and accountability questions into a single unsupported conclusion.

Paragraph source IDs: INC-P-005

OCHA also reported that a UNRWA school sheltering displaced people was struck in An Nuseirat Refugee Camp in June 2024. This row belongs in the chapter because it shows how the incident matrix handles shelter, displacement, and reporting language. The prose must keep casualty totals, combatant-status claims, and operational details source-labeled when the underlying record remains contested.

Paragraph source IDs: INC-P-001, INC-P-002, INC-P-004, INC-P-005

The chapter can use these incidents to show the method: a reconstruction is not a press release; an aid-organization statement is not a court order; an OCHA incident update is not a final criminal judgment. The record is strong enough to document what was reported, reconstructed, or recovered. It is not strong enough, on these rows alone, to assign individual criminal liability.

Unsafe wording to avoid: Do not convert a reconstruction, press investigation, aid-organization statement, or UN incident report into a final adjudication of intent or individual liability.

Section QA checklist:

- Keep the source type attached to every incident sentence.
- Use "reconstructed," "said," "reported," or "verified sample" only where that verb matches the row.
- Do not combine separate incidents into one composite scene.
- Add official investigative findings before escalating any liability language.

## 4. Legal Posture

Source rows: LAW-P-001, LAW-P-002, LAW-P-003, LAW-P-004, LAW-P-005

Paragraph source IDs: LAW-P-001, LAW-P-002, LAW-P-003

The legal record is publishable only if procedural posture remains visible. The ICJ ordered provisional measures in South Africa v. Israel; provisional measures are not a final merits ruling. The ICJ advisory opinion on the occupied Palestinian territory stated that Israel's continued presence is unlawful; the advisory-opinion posture remains part of the fact being reported. ICC public records describe warrants in the Situation in the State of Palestine; a warrant is not a conviction.

Paragraph source IDs: LAW-P-001

The ICJ provisional-measures row can support language about ordered interim measures and the existence of the case. It cannot support a sentence that the court has finally decided the merits unless the final merits record exists and is cited. The chapter should quote ordered measures exactly if it uses operative language.

Paragraph source IDs: LAW-P-002

The ICJ advisory-opinion row can support language about the court's advisory conclusion on the occupied Palestinian territory. The paragraph should name the advisory posture because that posture is not a technicality; it is part of what the legal record is. If the chapter discusses implementation, it must separate the advisory opinion from later political or diplomatic responses.

Paragraph source IDs: LAW-P-003

The ICC warrant-stage row can support language that public ICC records describe warrants in the Situation in the State of Palestine. It cannot support a conviction sentence. A warrant is a procedural record, not a final adjudication of guilt. Exact charges, named persons, and current posture should be checked against ICC public documents before publication.

Paragraph source IDs: LAW-P-004, LAW-P-005

UN Security Council and General Assembly records belong in the legal and diplomatic record, but they should not be collapsed into court findings. UNSC Resolution 2334, a General Assembly implementation resolution, a court order, an advisory opinion, and a warrant each carry a different legal or political meaning. The chapter can make those distinctions legible to a general reader without weakening the force of the underlying record.

Unsafe wording to avoid: Do not write that a court has finally adjudicated genocide, guilt, or individual conviction unless the cited record says that.

Section QA checklist:

- Use "provisional measures" for the ICJ interim order unless quoting exact operative language.
- Use "advisory opinion" for the ICJ occupied-territory record.
- Use "warrant" or "warrant-stage record" for ICC public records unless a later adjudication changes posture.
- Separate Security Council and General Assembly records from court records.

The editor should also preserve uncertainty where the source preserves uncertainty. Reported figures remain reported. Survey estimates remain estimates. Incident reports remain incident reports. The recommended structure is direct: start with the financial floor, move to reported humanitarian harm, show selected incident rows with evidence type attached, then close with legal posture. Do not start with the most emotionally charged incident and work backward to the sources. The source architecture should control the narrative sequence.
