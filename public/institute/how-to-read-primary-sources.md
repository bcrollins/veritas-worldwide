# How to read primary sources — Veritas Institute field guide

**Publisher:** Veritas Worldwide  
**Audience:** Researchers, students, journalists  
**Companion:** [Methodology](https://veritasworldwide.com/methodology) · [Sources library](https://veritasworldwide.com/sources)

## 1. Start with the document, not the narrative

Read the primary instrument (statute, filing, transcript, FOIA release, ledger) before secondary summaries. Ask: *who produced this, under what authority, on what date?*

## 2. Classify by hierarchy

| Tier | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Primary government / court | Statutes, CRS, PACER dockets, Federal Register, congressional record |
| Institutional / scientific | Peer-reviewed papers, official statistical series |
| Investigative with documents | Reporting that embeds or links primary exhibits |
| Contemporaneous reporting | Wire copy same day as event |
| Secondary commentary | Opinion, synthesis, social posts |

## 3. Apply Volume I evidence language

- **Verified** — multi-source or primary documentation.
- **Circumstantial** — documented facts with interpretive link.
- **Disputed** — claimed, not independently confirmed; retained for transparency.

The Record of Jesus Christ uses a seven-tier scholarly scale; map it carefully before mixing taxonomies.

## 4. Archive pins and access dates

Prefer Wayback / archive pins when origins block automated checks. Record access dates for every URL you rely on.

## 5. What not to do

- Do not treat forum upvotes or social virality as evidence weight.
- Do not present device/account identifiers as fact unless independently authenticated (see [Comprehensive Online Profile](https://veritasworldwide.com/comprehensive-profile)).
- Do not strip source context when quoting.

## 6. Practice sprint (30 days)

Week 1: pick one claim from The Record; open every linked primary.  
Week 2: rebuild the claim in your own words with hierarchy labels.  
Week 3: attempt a counter-read with alternative sources.  
Week 4: write a one-page methodology note.
